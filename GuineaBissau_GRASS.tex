\begin{abstract}
The objective of this study is to map land cover changes in the coastal region of Guinea-Bissau, West Africa. A study area encompasses the floodplains of the Geba, Caceu and Rio Grande de Buba rivers located in the western coastal region of Guinea-Bissau. Changes in the land cover types was assessed and compared. The methodology included the use of GRASS GIS scripts and modules "i.cluster" and "i.maxlik" for image analysis. Data include three Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS satellite images from 2017 to 2023 which were used for image classification and analysis. Auxiliary modules included "i.group", "r.import" and other GRASS GIS scripting techniques applied for Landsat image processing. The results of image processing demonstrated changes in land cover types that were observed, detected and compared for selected landscapes in the study area. The increase in the extent of mangroves and urban and agricultural areas and the corresponding decrease in natural forest areas are detected and demonstrated on the presented maps of land cover changes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Contexte}
Au cours des dernières décennies, des problèmes environnementaux à grande échelle, notamment la déforestation, l'érosion des sols et le surpâturage, ont attiré l'attention internationale sur la Guinée-Bissau. La conversion extensive des marais salants, des forêts et des mangroves en terres agricoles et urbaines a un impact sur les types de couverture terrestre dans les régions côtières du pays \cite{Lopes2022,RAIMUNDOLOPES2022106181,CABRAL2017115} et affecte la dynamique de la configuration du paysage \cite{VASCONCELOS2002139,LOPES2023116804}. Ce ne sont là que les effets les plus récents et les plus évidents des activités anthropiques dans cette région. 

Le changement de la couverture terrestre est devenu un sujet important pour la surveillance environnementale en raison des problèmes de changement climatique \cite{Palazot}. Diverses données de télédétection ont été utilisées pour détecter et surveiller les changements de la couverture terrestre à l’échelle locale, régionale et mondiale \cite{Dao}. L’objectif de la détection des changements est d’identifier les changements sur les images satellites entre deux dates ou plus, d’identifier le type de changement et de quantifier la quantité de ces changements. 

Un certain nombre de techniques de vision par ordinateur ont été développées pour une détection efficace des changements de la couverture terrestre \cite{Lenco,Mainguet}. Certains d’entre eux visent à détecter les changements de paysage à l’aide de techniques d’amélioration de l’image \cite{Bouchot}, telles que la différenciation des images, le rationnement ou les méthodes d’analyse des composantes principales \cite{Crettez,SHARMA2023100963}. Cependant, pour obtenir des informations détaillées sur les changements d’occupation du sol, la comparaison automatique de différentes images est nécessaire. Traditionnellement, la méthode détecte les changements détaillés de la couverture terrestre entre les images satellites à deux dates en comparant les cartes obtenues à l’aide d’une classification humaine supervisée \cite{Geroyannis,Blamont}. Cependant, l’utilisation d’une telle méthode nécessite des zones d’entraînement indépendantes et nécessite souvent la connaissance a-priori du terrain local.

Contrairement aux méthodes existantes, la classification par machine, présentée dans cet article, est indépendante et représente objectivement la dynamique du paysage basée sur les algorithmes de vision par ordinateur et la reconnaissance automatique de la réflectance spectrale au niveau des pixels. En alternative aux méthodes SIG existantes, cet article présente les images traitées et classées à l’aide de scripts, c’est-à-dire une approche de programmation appliquée au traitement des données géographiques. 

Dans cette étude, les principales transitions de classes de couverture observées étaient la perte de forêts de mangroves intactes et les gains correspondants en termes de superficie de zones agricoles et de croissance de végétation secondaire. Ces transitions correspondent aux modèles d'extraction de ressources prédominants dans les régions côtières de Guinée Bissau d'après les études pertinentes \cite{PEREIRA2022100746,CARREIRAS2012426}.

\subsection{Objective and motivation}

The main objective of this article is to address the aforementioned problem of environmental monitoring of Guinea-Bissau using the programming approach of GRASS GIS software for satellite image processing. A new series of Landsat OLI/TIRS images are processed using GRASS GIS modules and scripting strategy. Using this approach, deceleration of land cover types is done automatically using computer vision algorithms that detect the spectral reflectance of pixels in images and assign them to different categories by clustering. This method is tested, explained and presented in the form of a series of new maps covering the Guinea-Bissau region, West Africa, figure \ref{fig01}.

The specific objectives of this research were (1) to produce land cover maps based on the interpretation and classification of Landsat OLI/TIRS satellite photos from 2017 to 2023 covering 6 years, (2) to quantify and visualize dynamic changes. of vegetation cover based on these maps, and (3) assess the accuracy of digital classification for vegetation mapping in the floodplains and coastal zone of Guinea-Bissau. The methodology of this project draws on GRASS GIS analysis and scripting techniques used for processing a series of Landsat OLI/TIRS images. The images were digitally processed using algorithms for classification and visual interpretation of satellite images.

The aim of this research is to identify and quantify natural and human-induced changes in tidal floodplains in the coastal areas of the Geba, Caceu and Rio Grande rivers of Buba, Guinea-Bissau, using of Landsat OLI/TIRS satellite images from 2017 to 2023. The coastal region located in northwest Guinea-Bissau includes mangroves and floodplains, figure \ref{fig01}. These regions consist of various land cover classes and include flooded forests, grasslands, urban areas, secondary growth areas and mangrove forests \cite{ANDREETTA2016314}. The floodplains of river deltas along the Atlantic coast also include mangrove ecosystems.

\section{Study area}

The study area of this project is located in the western coastal region of Guinea Bissau, on the Atlantic coasts, approximately between latitudes 10.5° N to 13.0° N and longitudes 13.5° W and 17° W (figure \ref {fig01}). The coastal region of Guinea-Bissau is one of the least known tropical ecosystems in West Africa in the absence of existing studies.

Additionally, Guinea-Bissau is among the African countries most vulnerable and threatened by climate change due to its flat topography and large, winding coastal area consisting of several rivers flowing into the Atlantic Ocean, as well as ocean tides. which contribute to local flooding \cite{Temudoetal}. It is well known that changes in land cover types are linked to intensive agricultural activities and climatic effects \cite{TemudoSantos,Diengetal}, figure \ref{fig02}. Increasing temperatures in the interior regions of the country and periods of drought that lead to desertification.

\begin{figure*}[htb]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{Fig_02}
	\caption{Map of land use types in Guinea-Bissau, West Africa. Data: Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). Cartographic source: author. Software: QGIS.}
	\label{fig02}
\end{figure*}

Other effects include the effects of moons and tidal fluctuations along coastal regions which contribute to local flooding and increases in submerged vegetation such as mangroves. Thus, in Guinea-Bissau, temperature fluctuations and alternating monsoon-type rainy seasons, alternated with periods of hot, dry winds blowing from the Sahara, regulate the possibility of agricultural activities \cite{Carn}. A related analysis was also carried out regarding the ecological vulnerability of coastal regions of Guinea-Bissau and was reported by Lopes et al. (2022).

Guinea-Bissau's tropical climate experiences two extremely contrasting seasons: a dry season from November to May with dusty winds, hot, rainless weather, and a wet monsoon season from June to October with heavy rain and harsh conditions. hot and humid. This region is close to the mouth of the Geba River. Although it is not under the influence of the salt water of the ocean, it is affected by the tides characteristic of the Atlantic coast and the river estuary which affect the vegetation in the lagoons \cite{Catarino}.

\section{Satellite data}

The use of satellite images and remote sensing data began in the 1970s with the launch of \cite{Heymann} satellite missions. The summary of applications of satellite image processing in geographical studies since this period is well presented by Regrain \cite{Regrain}. The continued use of remote sensing data over the past 50 years for environmental monitoring proves their utility and unquestionable value as a data source for geographic research.

\begin{figure*}[htb]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{Fig_03}
	\caption{Color composite images over Guinea-Bissau, West Africa. Data source: USGS. The images were taken on May 7, 2023, May 6, 2020 and April 28, 2017.}
	\label{fig03}
\end{figure*}

Today, satellite images are the best available source of geospatial information and can be used to map landscapes and perform environmental analyses. For example, satellite images are used to analyze land cover changes and deforestation in Africa's tropical forests, and thus document their location and landscape changes over time \cite{Kilian,Lemenkova202310,jmse11040871,Tsayem }.

Among different types of satellite images, the importance of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS for environmental analysis has been well described in the literature. There are many examples of the use of Landsat for environmental monitoring of coastal regions of West Africa (Lahuec et al., 1992; \cite{Rifai,Lemenkova202227,Lemenkova202322}. Additionally, they are widely used for document the processes of deforestation, land cover and land use change in Guinea Bissau \cite{TemudoCabral,Cuq}.

Nevertheless, despite many existing use cases, remote sensing has limitations and inherent errors that can be introduced during pre-processing, numerical and visual classification, rectification to real-world coordinates, post-processing and recording on other images \cite{Desjardins,Girou}. For these reasons, it is necessary to use advanced image processing methods such as GRASS GIS software which allows to accurately process and analyze remote sensing data through special modules and scripts.

The segments of the Landsat color composite images from May 7, 2023, May 6, 2020, and April 28, 2017 used in this study are shown in figure \ref{fig03}. Images have been filtered to reduce noise due to cloudiness and atmospheric effects. Images were systematically selected from the USGS EarthExplorer repository and taken at the end of the dry season. During this period, cloud cover is generally low but vegetation appears bright green and contrasts with all other land cover types.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Using GRASS GIS for satellite image processing}

Landscape change detection analysis based on satellite images is a GRASS GIS software technique that performs a comparison between land cover types detected using images obtained on different dates. The comparative analysis of classification maps resulting from these multi-temporal data makes it possible to detect landscape changes \cite{Pennober,Ndehedehe2022}. 

\begin{figure*}[htb]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{Fig_04}
	\caption{True and false color composites of Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS images (bands 4-3-2, 2-3-4 and 7-6-4), Guinea-Bissau for the years 2017, 2020 and 2023.}
	\label{fig04}
\end{figure*}

In this approach, satellite images from different dates are classified by GRASS GIS independently and then the classification maps are compared for the years 2017, 2020 and 2023, figure \ref{fig03}. Such a comparison is based on individual pixel spectral reflectance values on images of different dates. Comparing these changes on classifying images from different dates shows the dynamics of the landscape over this period.

The digital image processing used to create a land cover map from Landsat OLI/TIRS imagery was carried out in five steps using GRASS GIS techniques: image import, data exploration and analysis, classification of images, image post-processing and mapping, and accuracy assessment. The Landsat images were geometrically rectified according to EPSG coordinates: 2095, Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) zone 28N for Guinea Bissau.

\subsection{Classification of Landsat data}

In general, accurate vegetation mapping using satellite image classification is not easy to achieve due to the spectral confusion between different vegetation types and the similarity in spectral reflectance of various plants. Additionally, the data is not normally distributed. Thus, in order to improve the classification accuracy, multi-temporal Landsat OLI/TIRS data were processed using a computer vision approach based on automated clustering techniques. This made it possible to classify different types of land use in Guinea-Bissau in the study area.

To facilitate data processing, the best combinations of Landsat bands were collected and stacked into false true and false color composite images (bands 4-3-2, 2-3-4, and 7-6-4 as Red -Green-Blue) and SWIR2, SWIR1, Red), figure \ref{fig04}. The bands that produced the best separation for rainforests, farmland, urban areas, coastal areas, and mangroves included bands 4 (red), 3 (green), and 2 (NIR). To process these bands, contrast stretching techniques and color composites were applied using the relevant modules of GRASS GIS software.

\section{Résultats}

On peut voir les différences distinctes entre les différents types de couverture terrestre ou d'utilisation des terres. Les surfaces d'eau et les régions côtières étaient assez lisses au moment de l'acquisition des images et apparaissent sous forme de couleurs sombres sur toutes les images. Le pourcentage de nébulosité est inférieur à 10\% pour toutes les images. De plus, les salines mélangées aux eaux douces des estuaires des rivières Geba, Corubal et Rio Grande de Buba ont des couleurs plus claires que celles de l'eau de mer. Les types de végétation tels que les mangroves, les forêts ou zones boisées de feuillus, les broussailles et les forêts de feuillus sempervirentes ont des signaux spectraux plus forts et apparaissent sous la forme de diverses ombres de vert, figure \ref{fig05}.

\subsection{Classification of Landsat data}

Major land cover types were visually distinguished before numerical classifications. These include the discrimination of mangroves, estuaries, urban areas and rainforests detected on color composites of Landsat images. Several derived bands were generated using color composites in GRASS SIG. The images were analyzed and compared using a spectral diagram and spatial analysis. The land cover map generated using unsupervised classification for images is shown in figure \ref{fig05}.

Flooded shrublands and deciduous forests along Atlantic coasts or estuaries appear dark green because their signatures differ from those of mosaic vegetation (e.g., semi-deciduous forests in inland regions of the country). They appear greenish and stand out from other land cover types. Mangrove plants partially submerged in water show no difference from the flooded vegetation of estuaries and are grouped in the same class. The light signature of needle-leaved, evergreen or deciduous shrubs in all images is attributed to the high backscatter coefficients of this vegetation type and spectral reflection effects. The accuracy assessment showing the probability of the correct assignment of the pixel to the correct class for the three images is presented in figure \ref{fig06} and calculated with averages for each calculated band.

Furthermore, it is demonstrated that the proposed techniques of GRASS GIS software have been successfully applied for land cover monitoring of Guinea-Bissau to analyze short-term landscape dynamics over the last five years (2017 -2023). This method can be extended and applied to other regions and periods. The image processing procedure using GRASS GIS software combines a module-based classification methodology to produce individual maps of land cover changes and the assessment of accuracy after classification for validation of the results. Clustering-based classification demonstrated the overall accuracy of the 2017, 2020 and 2023 Guinea-Bissau maps for convergence of 98.1\%, 98.2\% and 98.1\%, respectively. This demonstrated high mapping accuracy based on GRASS GIS processing of remote sensing data, figure \ref{fig07}.

By comparison of the classified maps, it is found that the cover of natural vegetation, including forests and mangroves, shrubs and grasses, has decreased while the second type of vegetation and built-up areas have increased. Based on the GRASS GIS method, changes in land cover types in the coastal region of Guinea-Bissau were detected and visualized in the target landscapes over the period from 2017 to 2023. Specifically, the images were compared to different dates to assess the dynamics. land cover types through the identification of various land cover patches.

The main land cover types identified included woodlands, mangroves, agricultural lands, mudflats and coastal and dryland wetlands. The areas covered by these land cover types were calculated and compared to assess the dynamics. Using digital calculations of the landscape fragments, the regression and progression of forests and mangroves were then assessed and reported in cluster reports that show which land cover types are expanding and which are decreasing. The results show that the areas of mangroves and drylands are increasing. The exact areas covered by these land cover types are reported in the relevant files and available in the author's GitHub with GRASS GIS scripts: https://github.com/paulinelemenkova/Guinea-Bissau-GRASS-GIS

Unsupervised automatic classification was performed with a variable number of clusters using the GRASS GIS module “i.cluster”. The results of the classification approaches for all images were evaluated by generating error matrices and running Kappa analysis to statistically determine the accuracy of the land cover maps, figure \ref{fig07}.

\section{Discussion}

The history of the landscape is our environmental heritage and our past reality. Understanding how land cover types change over time gives us insight into what happened and how human-environment interactions developed. The main threat of environmental change is linked to climate issues (desertification, erosion, landscape fragmentation, coastal degradation, deforestation). These and similar processes are accelerated by anthropogenic activities such as intensive agricultural activities and urban expansion.

This information can be derived from satellite images. Most of this information is hidden in remote sensing data and can be discovered once the images are processed and interpreted using spatial analysis tools. In this way, large amounts of information about landscape dynamics can be extracted from satellite images using GIS systems, as demonstrated in this article. Using different mapping techniques, we can reconstruct past landscapes, predict possible changes in the future and assess the rate of change in land cover types.

Digital image processing makes it possible to document environmental changes through mapping and tracking land cover types identified in the images. Different techniques are used for image processing and resulting mapping to document changes in land cover types. Some GRASS GIS modules support the technical parts of image processing, while others are intended for visualization and cartographic representation.

\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we have demonstrated that remote sensing and image processing provide the best way to keep  accurate record of landscape history and visualise land cover changes over time using time series analysis. Additionally, GRASS GIS software presents an accurate and advanced tool for environmental monitoring and mapping using numerous map visualization and image processing modules. We can conclude that remote sensing provides an effective support for environmental monitoring and thematic mapping using satellite images as a source of geospatial information.

An important issue for automatic machine-based classification by the GRASS GIS software approach is the independence of the field work and sampling tests. This allows for high-quality and reliable maps based on the classified images. This advantage becomes more crucial for regions with difficult access, such as the tropical forests of West Africa. Most regions of Guinea-Bissau do not have reliable maps of land cover types based on remote sensing data processing. Therefore, this article fills the existing gap in the existing literature by presenting a series of classified satellite images showing landscape dynamics in the coastal regions of Guinea-Bissau, West Africa.

In this study, we developed a satellite image processing programming methodology based on GRASS GIS software. The scripts are publicly available for reuse and reference on the author's GitHub repository with the classification results summarised for each image (2017, 2020 and 2023): https://github.com/paulinelemenkova/Guinea-Bissau-GRASS GIS are available in tables for each year. Through the implementation of the presented scripting technique, it is possible to achieve real-time change detection for environmental monitoring of African landscapes with an example of coastal region of Guinea-Bissau, West Africa.
